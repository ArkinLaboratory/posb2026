### Stop 3's epistemic tail — about 7 slides

After the four-step run that derives R₀ the deck spends eleven BUILD slides on
the paper's printed form against ours: what the printed version costs the
result, what the second count counts, which equilibrium, the assumption that
does not matter here, the machine that produced it, the other answer that is
also right, what that factor is, how firm the number is, where the boundary
came from.

This is good work and some of it is the best epistemics in the deck. It is also
the same lesson four or five times. **Keep the three that change what a student
would do** — the threshold in measurable things, how firm the number is, and
the other answer that is also right, which is the one that teaches that two
defensible R₀ definitions exist. The rest goes to the appendix, annotated.

### Stop 4's model-comparison tail — about 6 slides

Stop 4 establishes the hour's sharpest claim and **must keep the slides that do
it**: the shared knob, one knob at a time in D and in P, the derivative on its
own slide, and where the window ends up. That is the argument.

What follows it — what the model asks the cell to do, what the paper says about
that, the other model of the same cell, the capped model's number, which model
the evidence favors, twenty-one years later, the 1970 oscillation — is a
model-against-model comparison that the Limits column already carries in one
row. To the appendix.

**This is the stop to watch during the rebuild.** In session 24 the segment
with no graded technique was also the one serving no thesis, so it left whole.
Here the stop with no graded technique is where the hour's claim is
established. Cutting it by that rule alone would gut the argument. The tail
goes; the claim stays.
