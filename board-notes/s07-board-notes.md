# Board notes

Print this and carry it. The deck records what is *projected*; this records what
gets *written*.

**This session is not a board session.** Both derivations are step-slide runs.
The board carries **two ledger lines**, plus **one pair of empty axes** drawn at
minute 12 and never wiped — every figure today is that picture redrawn, and the
room should be able to look away from the screen and still see the axes.

**Conventions.** **ASK** — put it to the room before writing, and the answer to
expect. **POINT** — the moments you turn and point at the board rather than the
screen. **IF ASKED** — the question that always comes.

<div class="rule"></div>

## The board plan

Same room as sessions 3–6: blackboard across the front, screen down in the
**centre**, a **left wing** and a **right wing**.

**LEFT WING — the ledger. Two lines, written as they are derived, never wiped.**

**RIGHT WING — the axes at minute 12, and the vote tally at minute 9 above
them.** The tally is the only thing that gets wiped, and only at minute 26 when
you resolve it.

<div class="rule"></div>

## Right wing — minute 9, the vote tally

Four options, four tick columns, and the count in the room's sight. Write the
distribution down. Do **not** comment on it, do **not** hint, and do **not**
resolve it — the resolution is step 7 of derivation 1 at minute 26, and the
whole shape of the first half depends on the question staying open for
seventeen minutes.

**IF ASKED** *which is right?* — *"You are going to derive it. If I tell you
now, you will believe me instead of the algebra."*

<div class="rule"></div>

## Right wing — minute 12, the axes

Draw them once, large, and leave them:

- horizontal: **protein $p$**
- vertical: **rate (molecules per minute)**

Then two curves, in this order and no others: the **flat production line** and
the **straight removal line through the origin**. Nothing else goes on these
axes for the rest of the session; when you need a new picture, gesture at them
and let the slide carry the detail.

**ASK while drawing removal:** *why is removal a straight line through the
origin and not a constant?* Expect the answer from session 5 — dilution removes
a **fraction** per minute, so the amount removed grows with the pool. If nobody
has it, ask what happens to removal when $p = 0$.

**DO NOT WIPE.** You point at these axes at 32, at 50, at 69 and at 84.

<div class="rule"></div>

## Line 1 — at minute 24, step 6 of derivation 1

Left wing, and **box it**.

$$\text{speed-up } \lambda \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{cost } \lambda, \ \text{forever}
\qquad (\text{when } \lambda \text{ comes from } \gamma)$$

**POINT at the axes as you write it.** The tag moves the **removal** line — it
tilts it steeper — and the crossing slides down, so $\alpha$ has to rise to put
it back. Both effects are the same $\lambda$ because both live in the same
denominator.

**ASK:** *is that an approximation?* No. It is exact, and it is exact for the
same reason it is boring: one denominator, two requirements.

<div class="rule"></div>

## Line 2 — at minute 43, the last step of derivation 2

Directly under line 1.

$$\text{NAR} \;-\; \alpha \text{ raised},\ \gamma \text{ untouched}
\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{steady-state flux unchanged}$$

**Then turn to the axes.** This is the moment the session turns:

> The removal line never moved. The crossing is where the two curves agree, so
> the *rate* at the crossing is whatever removal says it is — and removal has
> not heard about your circuit.

**ASK:** *so negative autoregulation is free?* No — it is **prepaid**. The
transient costs more, the steady state costs the same, and which of those
dominates depends entirely on how long you run. That is item 4 of the handout
and the second rhythm at 50 min.

**IF ASKED** *does NAR really not change $\gamma$?* — not by itself. If you
build autoregulation by wiring in a protease adapter, you have built both
things, and then you pay both bills. Good question, and worth thirty seconds.

   minutes: keep "you have built a memory" and the growth-rate caveat, drop the

   distribution as well as speeding it up, that the mechanism is the same

   **Cut this one last, and read the note below before you do.**

> **Why variance moved from first cut to last, 14 September.** T17's assessment
> moved off PS3 and onto PS6 that day, so nothing between now and session 12

> on the ledger; variance is duplicated nowhere.

**Never cut:** the vote resolution at 26 min and line 2 at 43. Those two
