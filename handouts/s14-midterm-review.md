# Midterm — scope and preparation

**BioE 147 / BioE 247 · Midterm Thursday 15 October, in class, Dwinelle 219**

60 points, the full period. **One page of notes, both sides, prepared by you.** No
calculator — every numeric answer is one significant figure, and a bracketing
argument is a complete answer.

---

## Scope

**Sessions 1 through 11 at working depth.** You should be able to use this material,
not just recognize it.

**Sessions 12 and 13 by recognition only.** One item reads a quantity off a supplied
transfer curve, and nothing from those two sessions requires a calculation. You have
had one week and no graded practice there, and an exam that tested it at depth would
be measuring how fast you read.

Within sessions 1–11, the four things this exam asks you to do:

- **Reason about timescales and magnitudes.** Which of several processes governs a
  circuit's behavior and which can be neglected; whether a quantity someone hands you
  is dimensionally possible at all; when an approximation that eliminates a fast
  variable is legitimate.
- **Turn a mechanism into equations, and say what you would do with them.** Rate
  laws, stoichiometry, and the assembled system — plus what calculation would answer
  a question about it, described rather than carried out.
- **Read a dynamical picture.** Nullclines, fixed points and their stability, what
  the flow tells you, and what happens to the picture when a parameter is changed.
- **Argue about circuit function quantitatively.** Why a circuit is fast or slow,
  filters or pulses, oscillates or settles — and which parameter you would change to
  move it from one behavior to the other.

## What is not on it

No numeric signal matching between measured gates — that is PS6's job, and its
content *is* the computation, which does not fit in an exam period. No
delay-differential equations. No derivation reproduced from memory: where a criterion
is needed you may bring it on your page, and the credit is in using it.

---

## How it is graded, which should change how you answer

The paper is three parts: nine short items, three blocks built on one figure each,
and one longer setup item worth 10 points that asks you to build a system of
equations from a reaction list.

**On the short items and the blocks, the reason is where the points are.** A correct
answer with no reason earns half. "Diffusion is faster" is half an answer; "the
transcript takes tens of seconds and the protein crosses the cell in tens of
milliseconds" is the whole one. A few items are marked *no justification required* —
those ask only for a mark on a figure, a definition, or a calculation.

That has a consequence for your note page. **A page that is only a list of formulas
will not do much for you.** Every item that could be answered by copying a formula has
been written so that it cannot be. Notes that record *when* a result applies, what its
terms mean, and what assumption it rests on are worth considerably more than notes
recording what the result is.

## Preparing

The highest-value preparation is the problem sets you have already done, reworked
without looking at your solutions. PS1 through PS5 carry the computational fluency and
most of this exam builds on it rather than re-testing it — the exception is the
10-point setup item, where the mechanics are examined directly.

After that: for each of sessions 1–11, write down the one question that session
answers, and the one assumption its answer depends on. That second thing is what most
of the short items are about.

Bring your page.
